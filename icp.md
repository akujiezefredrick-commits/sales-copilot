# Ideal Customer Profile

## What we sell
- **Offer:** A done-for-you content marketing service for coaches and consultants. We plan, write and run their social media content, mainly on LinkedIn, so it brings in leads. The client doesn't have to create the content themselves.
- **Main outcome for clients:** A steady flow of inbound leads and booked sales calls from social media, built on the founder's personal brand and expertise.
- **Who we work with:** The CEO or founder, who is also the face of the business.

## Ideal customer
- **Business type:** Coaching or consulting businesses that sell their own expertise. For example: business, executive, leadership, sales, career, health or mindset coaches; strategy, marketing, operations, finance or HR consultants; fractional executives; advisors; small consultancies; coaching programs and masterminds.
- **Business model:** Sells high-value services, programs or retainers, where one new client is worth a lot. Growth depends on getting more sales conversations.
- **Company size:** Founder-led, usually a solo operator up to about 50 people. The founder is still the main source of expertise and trust.
- **Decision maker:** CEO, Founder, Co-founder, Owner, Managing Director or Principal.
- **Location:** Any English-speaking market.

## Signals of a strong fit
- The founder or company is **already posting** on LinkedIn or other social media, but not consistently or without a clear system. They believe in content but lack time to do it well.
- The website offers "book a call", "apply", discovery calls or programs, which shows they sell through conversations.
- There's a clear expert positioning or niche (who they help and with what).
- There are testimonials, case studies, a podcast, a book, speaking or a newsletter. They have expertise worth turning into content.
- There are signs they want to grow: new programs, hiring, launches, or posts about scaling.

## Weaker fit (score 4–6)
- A coach or consultant with no visible social activity at all. They may not value content yet.
- A larger consultancy (50–200 people) with some in-house marketing.
- An agency or other service business that isn't coaching or consulting, but is founder-led and sells expertise.

## Deal-breakers (score 1–3)
- Not a coaching or consulting business. For example: e-commerce, retail, restaurants, local trades, manufacturing, or software products with no expertise-led service.
- A large enterprise with its own marketing department or agency.
- A marketing or content agency that offers the same service as us, i.e. a competitor.
- Students, job seekers, or people with no real business behind them.
- No usable information on the website or LinkedIn at all. Score it low and say the data is missing.

## Examples
- **Great fit (9–10):** A founder-led executive coaching firm with 5 people. The website has "Book a discovery call". The founder posts on LinkedIn about once a month, and the company page shares client wins.
- **Good fit (7–8):** A solo fractional CMO consultant with a clear niche and testimonials, but little social activity.
- **Bad fit (1–3):** An online clothing store, or a 2,000-person IT consulting firm with an in-house marketing team.

## Scoring guide
- **9–10:** A coach or consultant, founder-led, sells high-value services through calls, and is already active or trying to be active on social media.
- **7–8:** A clear coach or consultant with one gap, such as little social activity, a slightly larger team, or unclear pricing level.
- **4–6:** A partial fit: a nearby type of service business, or a coach or consultant with weak signals of wanting to grow.
- **1–3:** Hits a deal-breaker, or the data is too thin to judge.

## Note for the icebreaker
Reps are speaking to the founder. The best icebreakers point to something specific the founder or company has said or done: a recent post topic, their niche, a program they launched, or a client result they shared.
